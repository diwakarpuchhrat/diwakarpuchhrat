CSE with AI Major
-----------------

I'm Diwakar, a computer science student at Bennett University specializing in AI/ML, and honestly still a little restless about what I want to build with it. Somewhere between coursework and 2 a.m. rabbit holes on arXiv, I've landed on the things that actually hold my attention: the internals of models, not just their outputs, and markets, not just as numbers on a screen but as systems with their own strange physics.

That combination shows up in how I pick projects. I don't gravitate toward the quick win or the polished tutorial clone. I'd rather sit with something that resists a clean answer. Right now that's ABYSS, a sonar-based system for spotting ghost nets and marine debris on the seafloor, where I'm running U-Net, ViT, and YOLO side by side instead of just picking a winner, because I wanted to actually see how they disagree. It's also NEOGENESIS, a self-directed graph-memory learning agent, and CANALIZATION, a research question I'm building on top of it about how LLM agents drift into narrow specialists over time. It's a question nobody handed me; I went looking for it.

Before any of that, I was doing graphic design, freelancing in Photoshop and Figma, and leading design for my college's entrepreneurship cell. I bring that same instinct for structure and clarity into how I build now, just pointed at architectures instead of layouts. I've also co-founded AgentForge, trying to get small businesses set up with the kind of web presence that used to be out of reach for them, and I've gone through a string of hackathons, including SIH and internal Bennett competitions, less for the trophies and more because a 36 hour deadline has a way of showing you what you actually know.

I'm working toward becoming an AI researcher or analyst, ideally somewhere the two threads of machine learning and quantitative finance actually meet. In the meantime, I'm doing the unglamorous parts: rebuilding my Python fluency after breaks, grinding DSA in C++, and reading papers most people skip past the abstract of. I'd rather be slow and right about how something works than fast and vague about it.

If you want the fuller trail, my GitHub has the code and my LinkedIn has the rest of the story.
